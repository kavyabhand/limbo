# 01 · The problem

> Hundreds of people are stuck in the same place with the same questions, and none of them can talk to each other.

## The moment

A trip goes wrong: a cancellation at 11 pm, a train held for eight hours in North Indian fog, a bus broken down on a
highway at night. Within minutes, everyone on it is asking the same things:

- What is actually happening, and for how long?
- Which desk or counter is the real one?
- Is there a rebooking, a refund, a hotel, a meal?
- How do I get to the city without being overcharged? Is anyone going my way?

The answers exist. They are scattered across the people standing in the queue: the one who heard the announcement,
the one who walked past the desk, the one whose app updated first. There is just no way to put them together.

## Why every existing tool fails at this

| Tool | What goes wrong in this moment |
|---|---|
| Airline or railway app | One-way, slow, often vague. No way to hear from other passengers. |
| PA announcements, departure boards | Easy to miss, one language, no detail. |
| WhatsApp or Telegram groups | Someone has to create the group and add people, and strangers don't have each other's numbers. When a group does exist, it's noise with no structure. |
| X or Reddit | Public, slow, not specific to your trip. |
| The people next to you | Useful, but you can only talk to the few within earshot. |
| Cab apps | Can't help you split a ride with someone from your own flight. |

## The root cause

People on the same trip share a **context** but have no **channel**. Nobody has the authority, the time or the
contact list to create one.

The people it hurts most are the ones least able to work around it: older travellers, people who don't speak the
local language, parents with children, anyone travelling alone at night.
